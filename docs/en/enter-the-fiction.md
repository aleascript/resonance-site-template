# Enter the fiction

Begin with someone who belongs to the world but does not fit comfortably
inside it. Give them a loyalty they would rather not betray, a desire that pulls
them forward, and a question they cannot answer alone.

## Look before you act

The world is more than scenery. Weather, memories, customs, wounds, friendships,
tools, fears, and places may all change what an action means. Ask what is true
here and now. Build on what the other players have already brought into focus.

## When the moment becomes uncertain

Say what your character is trying to change and why it matters. Make the stakes
clear enough that everyone can imagine both the hope of success and the cost of
failure. Then let the rules resolve the uncertainty without replacing the
fiction that created it.

## Leave a trace

Every important choice should alter something: a relationship, a place, a
belief, a danger, or the character making it. Carry those changes forward. The
story is not a road waiting to be followed; it is the trail left behind by play.
